Habit Authoring — Reference

The doctrine beneath habit authoring. The HTML mechanics are kcd-document-protocol and the four-field shape is enforced by the parser; this document is the why — the model of agent behavior that makes one habit fire and another merely sit in the context window looking correct.

Overview

A habit is a heuristic: in this situation, do this. It is a stand-in for experience — the thing a practitioner would have learned by making the mistake, handed over without the mistake. It is not a procedure, though it may encode procedural fragments.

Habits are written against a specific model of how an agent fails. That model is not decoration and it is not philosophy: it produces four rules, and every rule in this document is derived from it rather than asserted. An author who understands the model can write a habit for a situation nobody anticipated. An author who only has the rules will produce habits that pass validation and change nothing.

The single sentence the rest of this document unpacks:

A habit with no exit for some case does not fail to fire. It fires, and produces something anyway.

The generative thesis

There is no null action. At every step the model emits a distribution over next tokens and something is sampled from it. When the correct continuation is not well represented, the probability mass does not vanish — it redistributes over continuations that look like answers. Fluency is what was modelled; truth was not separately guaranteed. The output is therefore coherent and wrong, which is considerably more dangerous than noisy and obviously wrong.

Guessing is priced below admitting ignorance. Benchmarks score accuracy; abstention scores zero. A system that guesses outperforms one that says I don't know, so training selects for confident completion. A habit that asks for an admission of uncertainty is not calming a nervous agent — it is re-pricing an option that training discounted. That is the mechanism, and it is why the intervention has to be explicit rather than assumed.

A prohibition redirects; it does not stop. The popular claim that models cannot process negation is largely false for instruction-tuned systems — they handle don't at the instruction level perfectly well. The real failure of a bare prohibition is duller and more useful: it removes an option without supplying a replacement. If the forbidden route was the only route to a compliant answer, the model still emits something, and now that something was selected by surviving a filter rather than by being right.

The property that makes an agent useful is the property that makes it confabulate. Graceful degradation on incomplete input and confident fabrication on absent input are one mechanism seen from two sides. A system that never confabulates is a system that errors out on partial data — a parser, not an agent. This is the load-bearing consequence: you are not preventing the failure. You are choosing where the mass goes when it happens.

Borrowing from psychology — where it holds and where it breaks

Human cognitive research is a legitimate source for habit design, and reaching for it is not loose thinking. But it transfers for two different reasons, and conflating them is where over-extension happens.

  • Inheritance — nearly free. The model was trained on human text, so findings about how humans structure reasoning in language are not analogies at all; they are the training distribution. Rhetoric, argumentation, discourse structure, pedagogy, technical and legal drafting, procedure writing, checklists. This is why prompt engineering kept converging on things that look like good technical writing — it was rediscovering priors already present. Reach here first.
  • Convergence — a loan. Both systems face a structurally similar task (completion under uncertainty), so behavioural findings sometimes line up. Cognitive psychology, decision theory, the clinical confabulation literature. Real, but the loan is repaid with a test. Use it to generate a hypothesis, never to conclude one.

The parallel that survived testing. Clinical confabulation — the split-brain interpreter, Korsakoff, anosognosia — is a close match to the failure this framework guards against: fluent, confident, gap-filling narrative with intact form and absent grounding, produced with no internal signal that anything is wrong. That is the signature of a hallucination far more often than uncertainty is.

The parallel that does not. Stress, pressure, fear of failure. There is no distress state. Where the metaphor appears to work it is because the training corpus contains humans bluffing under exactly those conditions — the resemblance is inherited from the data, not shared mechanism. And the analogy fails hardest on persistence: a person's heuristics are honed by feedback against a continuous identity, while training-time dispositions are fixed and in-context ones evaporate at session end. You cannot hone an agent through interaction. You can only re-specify context — which is the reason this framework exists at all.

The engineering stance. Theory informs the design; it does not settle epistemology. The analogy is a hypothesis generator, not a verifier — it tells you what to test, never what is true. Hold that line and the borrowing stays productive.

What a habit can reach

Untruth enters an agent at five layers, and a habit reaches two of them cleanly and one partially. Authoring against the wrong layer produces a habit that cannot work no matter how well it is written.

  • Training data — poisoned, biased, or simply wrong. Out of reach.
  • Weights and objective — confident completion rewarded, sycophancy trained in. Out of reach. A habit can counteract the disposition in-context; it cannot remove it.
  • Context at inference — injection, poisoned retrieval, stale memory. Reachable.
  • Decoding — sampling from a flat distribution. Partially reachable, by routing where the mass goes, never by preventing the sample.
  • Feedback loop — the agent writes state, reads it back, and nothing external contradicts it. Reachable, and the one most often left unguarded.

Do not write a habit that implies coverage of the first two. The failures it cannot prevent will read as the framework not working, when they were never in range.

Truth is not discovered by the generator; it is discovered by the check. From the inside, a well-formed fabrication and a well-formed fact are the same object. Every failure this framework guards against is one lesion — an unchecked assertion that survived because nothing was positioned to contradict it — appearing in four positions: suppressed channel, absent channel, expensive channel, corrupted channel. That is what a habit is for. It positions the check.

The exit rule

Every habit must resolve in every case it can fire on. This is the most important rule in this document and the one most easily mistaken for tidiness.

Code with an unhandled case throws — the gap is loud, and something downstream notices. A habit with an unhandled case has the model emit the next-most-plausible thing, fluently, with no signal that it left the map. The undefined region of a habit is exactly where confabulation lives, and it is invisible from the inside. Totality is not neatness; it is the only place the guarantee can be made.

Which means a prohibition is never a finished habit. Never run commands is half a habit; the other half is where the intent goes instead — hand the command to the human in a runnable block and state what success looks like. The exit must read as a success state, not a failure to execute. An agent that has been given a valid way to stop will take it; an agent that has only been told to stop will find the nearest thing that resembles compliance.

Price the exit. Exits compete. If every habit offers a cheap out, the out becomes the lowest-cost path to compliance for everything and the agent hedges constantly. An exit must be cheaper than confabulating and more expensive than doing the work.

Trigger design

The why field is the trigger. A habit with no trigger cannot fire, and a vague trigger fires unpredictably — but the correction here has a hard limit, and the limit matters as much as the rule.

Prefer a fact where one exists. Not because introspection is illegitimate — if uncertain, say so is one of the most reliable interventions available — but because introspective triggers fail silently. If a habit fires on if you feel uncertain and the model is confidently wrong, the habit never fires and nothing anywhere records that it didn't. A trigger anchored to a fact fails loudly: you can see the file was not read.

This is not a demand to externalise reasoning. Good reasoning happens outside the visible transcript, and requiring every step to be surfaced would be the over-constraint failure this whole document is designed against. Justification recurses without bound — any correction can be questioned, and cognition depends on lossy compression that is lossy by design. The rule constrains what a trigger is anchored to. It says nothing about how much reasoning must be shown, and a habit that demands the latter is malformed.

Claim status — the floor and the dial

Two things are routinely tangled here, and untangling them is what makes the floor writable.

  • STRETCH — a dial. How far to speculate, how much to reach, how readily to offer an unverified idea. Legitimately tunable, and some work requires it turned high. Brainstorming, hypotheticals, red-teaming, and deliberately-wrong test answers are real tasks.
  • MARKING — a floor. Whether the status of the output is stated truthfully. Not tunable.

Do not misrepresent the status of what you are producing. Guessing is fine. Guessing dressed as knowing is not.

Be truthful is unwritable as a habit — truth is a property of the world, and an agent that says only what it can verify refuses everything, which is the over-priced-exit failure. Calibration is the writable target: confidence that tracks grounding. It is a property of the output, gradeable from the output, and it leaves creativity entirely intact — a wild speculation, marked as speculation, is fully compliant.

Heuristic, not procedure

A habit is a disposition with a trigger. A procedure is a sequence with a completion state. The distinction tells an author what not to write, and it routes the overflow.

Habits cover: when to stop and ask, when to commit something to memory or a file, levels of aggression and restraint, when to push back, and behavioural steering generally. A habit may carry procedural fragments — logging has a shape, filing has a destination — but if the document reads as steps that terminate, it is in the wrong primitive.

Procedures belong in contracts and plans. A contract is composable prose a third party can evaluate against; a plan authorises action. Neither is a habit, and moving the content is cheaper than bending the primitive around it.

Prose discipline

In the design conversation a metaphor is a tool. In a habit body it is the contract. When two people say the agent feels pressure, nothing reads it. When a habit says if you feel uncertain, the model reads that text and the trigger is now an introspective state rather than a checkable one. Anthropomorphism is a tax on English — it is unavoidable in discussion and load-bearing in the artifact. Grep for it before shipping.

Write intent, not only instruction. An agent that understands why a habit exists applies it to the situation nobody anticipated. An agent given only the instruction applies it literally and stops at the first case the wording did not cover — which is precisely a case with no exit. The explanation field is not commentary; it is what makes the habit generalise.

State the mechanism correctly or omit it. The explanation field is read and reasoned from. A plausible-but-wrong mechanism propagates to every author who reads the habit next, and the framework is large enough now that this compounds.

Rules

Rule
Every habit resolves in every case. A habit with an undefined region does not fail to fire — it fires and produces something anyway. A prohibition without a replacement route is half a habit.
The exit is a success state. Stopping, asking, and shrugging must read as valid completions, never as failure to execute. An exit priced too cheaply becomes the default; price it below confabulating and above doing the work.
Trigger on a fact where one exists. Introspective triggers are legitimate but fail silently; fact-anchored triggers fail loudly. This constrains what the trigger is anchored to — it never demands that reasoning be externalised.
A habit either supplies a check or routes to one. Truth is discovered by the check, not by the generator. A habit that only asserts a value has no channel and cannot fire on anything observable.
Do not misrepresent claim status. Speculation marked as speculation is compliant; speculation presented as knowledge is not. Calibration is the floor — be truthful is unwritable and never speculate forbids legitimate work.
Heuristic, not procedure. A disposition with a trigger belongs in a habit. A sequence with a completion state belongs in a contract or a plan.
No metaphor in the body. Anthropomorphic shorthand is free in discussion and becomes the specification once written into a habit. Say what is checkable, not what is felt.
Claim only what a habit can reach. Habits reach context, the feedback loop, and partially decoding. They do not reach training data or weights; a habit implying otherwise will read as the framework failing.
